The Industrial Revolution (c. 1760-1840)

The Industrial Revolution began in Britain in the second half of the 18th century. It was the shift from hand production in homes and workshops to machine production in factories.

Causes: Britain had coal and iron ore, a growing population, colonial markets, banks willing to lend, and a stable government. The agricultural revolution produced more food with fewer workers, so many people moved to towns.

Key Inventions and Effects

Key inventions: the spinning jenny (James Hargreaves, 1764), the steam engine improved by James Watt (1769), the power loom (Edmund Cartwright, 1785) and the steam locomotive (1804).

Effects: rapid growth of cities (urbanisation), factory work with long hours, child labour, and pollution. Over time, reforms such as the Factory Acts limited working hours for children. Living standards rose in the long run, and trade unions began to form.
